Traditional two-dimensional (2D) waveform direction of arrival (DOA) estimation relies primarily on the non-structured sample covariance matrix. However, when estimating the covariance matrix from a limited number of observations, it often fails to perfectly reflect its Toeplitz and Hermitian structural characteristics. To address this, we propose a high-precision interference localization method that preprocesses the covariance matrix. Firstly, for two-dimensional coarray signals, we introduce a second-level Toeplitz (2LT) structure matrix and derive it based on the elements of the Toeplitz matrix; secondly, we parameterize the second-level Toeplitz matrix, drawing on the technique of structured covariance estimation, to obtain a closed-form solution; finally, we reconstruct the noisy extended covariance matrix and employ a two-dimensional DOA estimation method. Simulation results show that the structured covariance matrix can enhance the performance of two-dimensional DOA estimation for interference signals based on sparse arrays. Specifically, under different signal-to-noise ratios and increasing numbers of interference devices, the proposed estimator consistently approaches the Cramer-Rao bound (CRB) in terms of root mean square error (RMSE).
Introduction Interference poses a serious threat to wireless networks, exploiting the openness of communication media to cause service disruption [1]. By utilizing receivers that operate only in the receiving mode, passive functions such as monitoring and analyzing the incident signal can be achieved. In this scenario, the use of two-dimensional (2D) DOA estimation technology allows for the localization of interference devices without requiring any transmission from the receiver. This is particularly valuable in scenarios where stealth or silent signal transmission is crucial.
To avoid interference attacks, the authors of [2] used rotation-invariant techniques for signal parameter estimation (ESPRIT) and multi-signal classification (MUSIC) algorithms to estimate the direction of arrival (DOA) of interference signals using uniform linear arrays (ULAs) and L-shaped array antennas. In [3], the authors employed a fast orthogonal search method combined with an improved interference signal detection effect by uniform circular arrays (UCAs). Additionally, in [4], the use of uniform linear and circular arrays was utilized to estimate the direction of coherent interference sources. However, these uniform arrays face significant challenges due to their limited aperture and degrees of freedom (DOFs). Consequently, the authors of [5] exploited the augmented receive covariance matrix from virtual differenced arrays to estimate the one-dimensional (1D) DOA of multiple interference signals. This study focused solely on estimating 1D DOA. Subsequently, [6] considered the use of two-dimensional sparse arrays and sparse signal processing for interference source direction finding and suppression. It is evident that precise DOA estimation can achieve more accurate positioning of signal sources, thereby enhancing the system's ability to distinguish and detect interference signals.
This paper investigates the application of two parallel arrays in two-dimensional direction of arrival (DOA) estimation. Related research compares the core ideas, main tools, and differences of partial two-dimensional arrays. Some studies have used the two-dimensional propagation operator method (PM) and multi-signal classification estimation methods on uniform linear arrays and uniform circular arrays. However, if there are N physical sensors in these uniform arrays, their degrees of freedom are only O(N). Therefore, subsequent research proposes sparse nested arrays and interleaved arrays, which can construct a differential coarray with O(N²) virtual elements by only deploying N physical sensors. Subsequently, these CAB-based models employ root multisignal classification (RMUSIC) and rotationally invariant techniques for signal parameter estimation algorithms to identify signals beyond the number of physical sensors. Additionally, grid size poses a significant challenge in DOA estimation: coarse grids lead to estimation errors, while fine grids significantly increase computational complexity. In addition to the aforementioned methods, deep learning technology has also been widely applied, but due to its high computational resource requirements, this paper does not involve it. Overall analysis shows that the proposed unstructured method can reduce computational complexity and enhance effective degrees of freedom.
Recent research results indicate that even when the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) approaches infinity, estimation errors converge to a positive value rather than zero, a phenomenon known as "saturation". In fact, the covariance matrix estimated directly from samples is merely an approximation of the true covariance matrix. Research indicates that algorithms based on structured covariance matrix estimation can improve angle estimation performance over those relying on sample covariance matrix. Therefore, many methods relying on covariance matrix estimation have received widespread attention, but these methods mainly focus on one-dimensional estimation and uniform array configurations.
Based on the above background, this paper proposes a new two-dimensional direction of arrival (DOA) estimation method based on structured covariance matrix estimation. It employs two parallel and mutually incompatible arrays to locate interference devices. Unlike traditional methods based on coarrays, the algorithm derived from the second-order Toeplitz (2LT) structure to derive the enlarged covariance matrix and constructs a second-order Toeplitz matrix within the virtual coarray domain. It is important to note that the Hermitian - Toeplitz matrix can be considered as a function of the unknown parameter vector. Therefore, starting from the sample covariance matrix, this paper derives the closed-form solution for the second-order Toeplitz structure estimation, followed by an eigenvalue decomposition (EVD) of the estimated noisy enlarged covariance matrix. This method effectively extracts the DOA of the interference signal, overcoming the "saturation" problem present in the DOA estimation based on coarrays using unstructured sample covariance matrices.
Conclusions Different from traditional two-dimensional direction of arrival estimation methods based on the enlarged covariance matrix, this paper adopts two parallel and mutually incompatible arrays to fully utilize the second-order Toeplitz matrix structure. By introducing the asymptotic maximum likelihood (ML) estimation of structured covariance matrices, it extends the estimation method to the field of two-dimensional direction of arrival estimation, aiming to obtain the noiseless version of the enlarged covariance matrix under a virtual array. Through the optimization of the second-order Toeplitz covariance matrix structure, the proposed DOA estimator outperforms traditional two-dimensional direction of arrival estimation methods based on sparse arrays.
